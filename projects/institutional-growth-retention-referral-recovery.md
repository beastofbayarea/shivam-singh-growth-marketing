# Rebuilding institutional trust while an emerging-markets strategy changed regime

A reported $5 billion emerging-markets strategy had two products in distress.

The investment process kept buying declines as markets became more persistent and liquidity fragmented; execution slippage moved from 5 to 50 basis points. Clients could see performance but not the regime, risk drivers, liquidity, or decisions behind it. Seventy percent of departing clients reportedly gave no explicit warning.

At D. E. Shaw, I built the relationship-recovery system with institutional clients, relationship managers, Portfolio Management, Quant Research, Risk, Execution, Investor Relations, Compliance, Product/Technology, and leadership. Investment teams kept model/capital authority; the evidence, warning, communication, and referral journeys connecting portfolio facts to client action were my remit.

## Two recovery loops ran in parallel

### Investment loop

Portfolio teams introduced circuit breakers and manual risk reduction, then evaluated a regime architecture spanning mean reversion, trend, and capital preservation under explicit risk budgets. It ran in shadow before a limited $500 million Brazil/India deployment.

Red-team review found look-ahead leakage: an early backtest normalized using future crash information. The team removed it and moved to walk-forward testing—train on prior periods, test the next unseen window, repeat.

I did not claim authorship of the model. I made the validation failure, remediation, limits, live authority, and observed behavior understandable to client teams. No backtest entered a client narrative until model risk and portfolio owners approved its meaning.

The [IMF July 2018 outlook](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2018/07/02/world-economic-outlook-update-july-2018) documents rising yields, trade tensions, capital-flow pressure, and currency stress. It supplies market context, not private performance evidence.

### Relationship loop

I combined permitted account, liquidity, portal, content, service, meeting, contact-gap, exposure, and relationship signals to prioritize human outreach. The signal never decided redemption. Relationship managers saw its factors, added context, dismissed noise, and selected the appropriate specialist.

The process reportedly moved outreach 21 days earlier. The defensible measure is first qualifying risk signal to first contact for comparable eventual-risk accounts; earlier contact is an intervention window, not proof of retention.

## The portal answered allocator questions

Instead of showing a confidence score, the client view surfaced:

- current regime and uncertainty;
- portfolio/market contributors to risk;
- active posture and circuit-breaker state;
- liquidity and relevant limits;
- bounded scenarios with assumptions; and
- the evidence that would change the next decision.

Severe oil, currency, liquidity, and correlation scenarios were first used in relationship-manager meetings. Impossible inputs were blocked; plausible outputs were labeled simulations, not forecasts. Proprietary source code stayed protected while decision logic and consequence became inspectable.

[FINRA Rule 2210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2210) provides a fair, balanced, specific communication benchmark where applicable; the record does not establish its coverage of every entity or communication.

## Questions became the growth backlog

Client questions were classified by root cause: missing market explanation, unclear liquidity, portal confusion, scenario request, delayed follow-up, or strategy concern. Repetition changed the product or cadence rather than generating another generic email.

Referral entered only after current evidence and recovery conversations. Asking for advocacy during unexplained loss would have converted trust repair into a sales tactic.

A “$12.75 per dollar” ROI cannot be reconstructed and appears to mix retained/referred assets with value. I exclude it.

Defining at-risk relationships, permitted signals, human escalation, portal requirements, communication architecture, question taxonomy, retention/referral sequence, evidence cadence, and metric boundaries was my growth responsibility. Investment/Risk/Execution controlled strategy; relationship managers controlled advice; Compliance approved communication; Finance controlled flows/fees.

The strategic growth capability was a closed loop: changing markets altered portfolio action, changing client behavior altered relationship action, and repeated client questions altered the product. Trust came from inspectable operating evidence, not reassurance layered over performance.
